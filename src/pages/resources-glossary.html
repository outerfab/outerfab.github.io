Chip-on-board glossary

Short definitions of the terms used across this site, in alphabetical order. Each links to the page that uses it.

Backgrinding
Thinning a wafer from the back before dicing, when the die must be thinner than the wafer. See wafer dicing and division.
Ball bonding
A wire bonding method that forms a ball at the end of the wire and presses it onto the pad, used with gold wire. Compare wedge bonding.
Bond finger
See PCB bond pad.
Bond pad
A pad a wire is bonded to. Die pads are on the die; PCB bond pads, also called bond fingers, are on the board. See bond pads and pitch.
Bonding diagram
A drawing of the die on the board with a line for each wire from a die pad to a PCB pad. It tells the bonder which pad goes where.
COB (chip-on-board)
A bare die glued directly to a printed circuit board and wire-bonded to it, then covered with glob-top. There is no package. See chip-on-board bonding.
Die
The bare, unpackaged chip, cut from a wafer. The plural is die.
Die attach
Fixing the die to the board with adhesive, before wire bonding. The pad it sits on is the die attach pad.
ENIG, ENEPIG
Surface finishes for PCB pads: electroless nickel with immersion gold (ENIG), and with palladium added (ENEPIG). Both are suitable for wire bonding. HASL and OSP are not.
ESD structure
Protection circuitry on a die's pads. We use it to test signal pads: a small current through the structure shows whether the bond is connected. See how we test.
Fiducial
A copper mark on the working edge of a panel that the machine uses to locate it. At least two must stay visible after die attach.
Film frame
A frame holding dicing tape with singulated die on it. One of the ways die can be supplied. See supplying your die.
Glob-top
The epoxy dome over the die and its wires. Ours is hard AB epoxy, clear or black.
Jig
The metal fixture that holds boards or panels on a bonding machine. Fit and dimensions depend on the partner and process; review the panel before ordering.
KGD (known-good die)
Bare die tested at wafer level and sold as working. See known-good die sourcing.
Mil
One thousandth of an inch, 25.4 µm. Wire diameter is often given in mils: 1.0 mil is 25.4 µm.
MPW, shuttle
A multi-project wafer run, where many designs share one wafer to share the cost. Tiny Tapeout and wafer.space run shuttles.
Open
A fault where a wire does not connect the die pad to the board pad. One of three faults we test for.
Panel
Several boards joined into one PCB and separated after assembly. See arranging boards into a panel.
Pitch
The distance from the centre of one pad to the centre of the next.
Pin 1
The reference pad that defines the orientation of a die. Mark it on the pad map and the bonding diagram.
Short to ground
A fault where a wire or pad touches ground when it should not.
Short to neighbour
A fault where two adjacent wires touch each other.
Singulation, dicing
Cutting a wafer into individual die.
Slot size
The die size on a wafer.space shuttle: 1 x 1, 1 x 0.5, 0.5 x 1 or 0.5 x 0.5.
Tape and reel
Die in the pockets of carrier tape wound on a reel. See supplying your die.
Waffle pack
A tray with one pocket per die. Our standard supply format.
Wedge bonding
A wire bonding method that presses the wire flat under a wedge-shaped tool, used with aluminium wire. It is our standard, at 1.0 mil. Compare ball bonding.
Working edge
A component-free strip along two opposite edges of a board or panel that the machine holds. Also called a rail. It carries the tooling holes and fiducials.
